About me.

I'm Adil Rasheed, a Computer Science and AI student who likes understanding how things work then building things from that understanding. I’m especially drawn to problems where technology, people, and complex systems overlap.

I spend most of my time moving between learning and making. That might mean studying AI or software architecture, exploring ideas around cognition and decision-making, or turning a problem I’ve noticed into a product, prototype, or experiment.

Long term, I want to build ambitious technology that meaningfully changes how people live and work. Along the way, I’m trying to become better at understanding both sides of that equation: the systems we build, and the humans they are built for.

Current interest

Where the human ends and the AI begins.

I'm interested in the boundary between human intelligence and artificial intelligence. As AI becomes increasingly capable for acting on our behalf, I think one of the most important questions becomes: where should the human end and the AI begin?

Right now, I'm particularly interested in systems where AI expands human capability without quietly replacing human agency. The machine can explore possibility spaces, surface patterns, and reduce the cost of execution. But deciding what matters, what to believe and what risks are worth taking should remain fundamentally human.

I'm also fascinated by what happens as this boundary moves beyond software and into the physical world: systems that can understand environments, retain experience, and help humans make increasingly complex decisions.

The future I find most interesting is therefore neither fully human nor fully autonomous, but one in which we become much more precise about the comparative advantage of each. AI should expand the field a human can perceive and act upon. The human should remain responsible for choosing where to go.
